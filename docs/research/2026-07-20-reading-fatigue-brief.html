Bottom line up front

The answer to too much text is not shorter text — it is less text existing in the human channel at all. Every mature field that solved human overload from automated systems (aviation, medicine, nuclear, SRE, intelligence briefing) solved it structurally: delete nominal output, tier the channels, retrieve just-in-time, and demand decisions instead of reading. Compression — the caveman strategy — is conspicuously absent from every field that actually solved this. Your instinct was right: caveman is a step, not a destination.

The portfolio below starts with pure deletion (roughly 3× fewer human-facing words per feature, zero grokking cost), then rebuilds trust structurally: a 600-word human-owned theory of the system, an external verifier for documents, and one decision-forcing checkpoint at design time.

01

Why reading fatigue happens — six mechanisms

Mechanism 1

A throughput mismatch no discipline can close

Careful reading caps at ~250–300 wpm; your 186k-word corpus is ~12 hours of continuous reading before any comprehension effort. Review effectiveness collapses past ~400 lines per sitting — and artifact size, not reviewer ability, is the lever. "Read more carefully" is not an available move.

Rayner et al. 2016 · SmartBear/Cisco · Baum et al., EMSE 2019
Mechanism 2

Complacency gets worse as the harness improves

Highly reliable automation induces reduced monitoring in experts as much as novices, and practice does not fix it. The cruelest finding for Joycraft: a maturing harness mechanically increases rubber-stamping. Any solution built on passive consumption — however short — decays.

Parasuraman & Manzey 2010 · Mackworth vigilance studies
Mechanism 3

The code/docs asymmetry is trust calibration, not laziness

You stopped reading code in harnessed projects because code has an external verifier (tests, CI) — that's calibrated reliance, and it's legitimate. Docs have no verifier at all; human reading is the only check. That's exactly why the fatigue concentrated in documents. (And why firmware still gets read: no adequate verifier there either.)

Parasuraman & Riley 1997
Mechanism 4

The firehose is prescribed — and mostly never on a read path

The same spec table is mandated in 4 places; 81 files carry identical 66-word boilerplate; 43 of 61 legacy specs were never touched after creation; 29 of 43 feature folders are briefs that were never built. Reading value is inversely proportional to volume in your own repo.

Internal audit, this study
Mechanism 5

LLM prose is a persuasive artifact

HumanLayer's Dexter Horthy publicly recanted the read-plans-not-code workflow after it failed at scale three times: "plans are persuasive artifacts by nature." Polished agent prose invites acknowledgment, not scrutiny. "Comprehension debt" is the result — and it lives in the human, not the corpus.

Horthy/HumanLayer RPI→QRSPI · Osmani, Comprehension Debt
Mechanism 6

Reading is for theory-building — and chronicles don't build theory

The deliverable is your mental model of the system (Naur). Skimming preserves gist and discards detail; narrative session summaries deliver activity-proportional text when what you need is model-delta-proportional text. Caveman compresses the narration; it cannot change what the narration is for — which is why it feels insufficient.

Naur/Goedecke · NNGroup F-pattern · Fregnan et al. 2022

Four scenarios where the fatigue bites

Sprint 4, 61 specs deep — the batch-return

You kick off /joycraft-implement-feature on an 8-spec queue and go to lunch. You return to a scrolled-off transcript, ~7,600 words of fresh specs you'll never open, a session-end narrative, and a queue JSON that may or may not agree with the frontmatter. Twelve hours of backlog reading isn't a task — it's a guilt object. You skim nothing carefully, approve everything, and your mental model quietly diverges another increment.

The rubber-stamped design review — the HumanLayer failure

A gnarly upgrade-path feature. joycraft-design hands you a fluent 200-line design doc. Buried in paragraph six is a confidently wrong assumption about where state lives. The prose reads well; you read fifteen lines, think "looks right," and approve. The assumption propagates into 8 internally consistent specs, agent-written tests encode it, verify passes (its oracle is the spec itself), and the bug ships as a coherent, fully-verified misreading of your intent.

The undead backlog — ambient guilt

Friday afternoon. docs/features/ holds 43 folders; 29 are briefs that were never decomposed. Only 5 of 35 briefs have status frontmatter, so no skill — and no human — can tell live from dead. Every directory glance, every agent context-gather, every "where were we on X" pays a tax on 100k+ words of abandoned intake. The corpus isn't just unread; most of it was never meant to be read, and nothing distinguishes it from the parts that were.

Two weeks away — cold re-entry

You return from a break or context-switch back from another project. Your options today: archaeologically skim 43 feature folders, re-read a decision log that's grown 14× past its template, or ask the agent and hope. What you actually need is one page: what the system is now, what changed while you were gone, what assumptions are live. No artifact in the current harness is that page.

The portfolio — ranked, panel-tested

All 10 original proposals were revised under attack; none advanced untouched, none was killed outright — but 12 individual components died (see the graveyard). Detail sits behind each disclosure, deliberately.

#1

Exhaust Deletion & Dedup — spec cards + the Reaper

Ship now

Delete prescribed redundancy at the template level (the 4-way spec table, the Test Plan that restates ACs 1:1, 81 copies of TDD boilerplate) and sweep the 29 undead feature folders into a greppable archive. Roughly 3× fewer human-facing words per feature — the only intervention every attacker conceded has zero grokking cost.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

Collapse the 4-way spec-table bookkeeping to the queue JSON. Kill the Test Plan table. Move the boilerplate once into joycraft-implement.md (agent-read, not human-read). Replace brief-content-copied-into-every-spec with one per-feature context.md. Restructure specs as ~300-word fact-box cards with mandatory Constraints/Approach/rejected-alternative fields and a visible "cut under budget" residue line. The Reaper: backfill status frontmatter, sweep undead folders to docs/archive/ — excluded from agent context and PR review via the existing .gitattributes machinery, never git-history-only deletion.

Residual risks

Caps are gameable. The panel killed hard enforcement ("cut, do not ask" makes omissions invisible) — cutting must always leave visible residue, MUST/MUST-NOT constraints are never cut, and high-risk specs get an uncapped body. Skill edits fan out to ~24 synced files across three harness variants; same-commit bundle-regen discipline required.

One-week experiment

Two days: edit the decompose template (delete Test Plan, add card format + residue line), run it on one real feature, compare word counts and your honest review time against the last comparable feature. One afternoon: hand-run the Reaper on docs/features/ and see if anyone misses anything within the week.

#2

Theory Ledger

Build this quarter

A ≤600-word, human-owned theory of the system (docs/context/theory.md) plus at most 7 delta lines per session, confirmed at the PR bookend by restating them in your own words. The panel's judges called it "arguably the strongest single idea" — it targets where comprehension debt actually lives: your mental model, not the corpus.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

Four sections — Components / Data flows / Invariants / Live assumptions — cap enforced mechanically (your decision-log grew 14× past its template; soft budgets fail here). Session-end emits NEW/REVISED/EVICT delta lines, each with a deterministic anchor (file:line, test name, grep command) and a decay date. REVISED/EVICT lines require you to restate them — generation, not recognition. After N silent sessions, an independent verify-pattern subagent re-derives the theory from code and diffs it against the ledger. Dual-use payoff: 600 human-vetted words beat 132k words of unverified exhaust as agent context.

Residual risks

It's a re-entry index and reading-by-exception mechanism, not a theory transplant — working with the system remains the source of theory. "Theory unchanged" is a claim, not a report; the periodic verifier audit is what distinguishes silence from a missed delta, and it is not optional. A rubber-stamped wrong line contaminates every future agent session from a single point of truth.

One-week experiment

Hand-write theory.md for Joycraft itself in one sitting — this tests whether 600 words can carry the system at all. Add a 10-line delta step to session-end. Run two real sessions and see whether the deltas are the things you actually wanted to know.

#3

Intent Tripwire + doc hygiene

Build this quarter

Give documents what code already has: an external verifier. Deterministic npx joycraft check-docs for consistency drift, plus the core rule — every acceptance criterion must trace to a line in a human-read design doc; an untraceable AC is a red "NEW CLAIM" exception that pages you exactly where requirement drift enters.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

Layer 1 (shippable now): queue-JSON vs frontmatter vs brief-checkbox consistency, Affected-Files path existence, undead-brief flagging — advisory first. Layer 2: AC-to-design traceability, and joycraft-verify's oracle repointed from the spec to the human-approved brief + the project's ALWAYS/NEVER boundaries, so at least one sensor is independent of the artifact chain it grades. Three separate proposals independently converged on "repoint the verifier to the brief" — the strongest convergence signal in the whole panel. Every red arrives as a 3–5 sentence model-diff, never a bare refutation.

Residual risks

AC-to-test mapping is prospective-only (existing Test Plans are free prose). Semantic checks remain LLM-judging-LLM: verdicts must say "unrefuted," never "verified." The reading license is precise: this excuses spec bodies post-approval and bookkeeping — it does NOT excuse the design doc or ACs, which remain the intent contract.

One-week experiment

Day one: write the three deterministic checks as a standalone script and run it on the current corpus — the drift counts it finds are the business case. Day two: hand-trace the ACs of one shipped feature back to its design doc and count untraceable ones. If >0 on the first try, the tripwire has already justified itself.

#4

Deposition Checkpoint

Pilot · 4 weeks · kill criterion

Replace the doc-reading checkpoint with a forced-choice interrogation: ≤5 risk-ordered questions derived from the design doc's decisions, plus a one-screen "assumptions asserted as fact" manifest. Reading becomes deciding — the one mitigation the complacency literature says survives.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

The design doc still gets written (git-native, team-legible). From its Resolved Decisions and Open Questions it derives ≤5 forced-choice questions via the native question UI, each with a "reject this framing" escape. Integrity: anything touching an ASK FIRST/NEVER boundary becomes a mandatory question regardless of agent confidence; the assumptions manifest gets one human glance; session-end randomly spot-reads one generated spec per feature. Each answer requires a one-sentence typed rationale (actual generation). Answers land as decision-log rows injected into Hard Constraints.

Residual risks

The deepest unfixed hole in the portfolio: the reviewee still filters the review channel — a premise the agent is confidently wrong about scores zero uncertainty and may not surface even in the manifest. The boundary bypass and spot-read narrow this, not close it. Question quality will drift without a minimal Tolan-style eval (seeded designs with known-dangerous buried decisions).

One-week experiment

No code: on the next real feature, after design produces its doc, prompt the agent by hand to extract the 5 highest-stakes decisions as forced-choice questions plus an assumptions manifest. Answer them, time it, and check whether anything the questions missed bit during implementation. If you bypass to free chat by the second feature, kill the format and keep only decision-stamping into specs.

#5

Report-by-Exception + attention policy + /recall

Ship as prompt-level guidance

Nominal spec = one line emitted by the independent verifier, never the implementer. A hand-owned DARK/SAMPLE/FULL attention-policy table makes "permission to not read" an explicit contract instead of guilt — with the firmware instinct preserved as a written carve-out. joycraft-recall answers known questions in ≤150 cited words.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

Deviations are BLUF, risk-ordered, one screen, with approve/reject/show-me. ~1-in-5 nominal specs randomly surface a full report so the false-negative rate stays observable. The policy table has hard rules: intent prose is never DARK; NEVER-boundary paths are always FULL. No adaptive calibration — tier changes are manual, driven by escaped defects. Every mature overload field converged on exception-only channels and tiering; the panel endorsed the direction unanimously once silence had to be earned by an independent sensor.

Residual risks

This is prompt-level aspiration, not an ISA-18.2 guarantee — verbosity discipline decays with model updates, and the TUI streams scrollback Joycraft doesn't control. The first hidden regression costs trust in the dark tier; the 1-in-5 sample is the only early-warning system and is load-bearing. Unknown-unknown drift is the Theory Ledger's job, not this one's.

One-week experiment

Edit the output sections of session-end/spec-done with the exception philosophy, hand-write a 10-row attention-policy table in 15 minutes, run one sprint. Measure nothing; just ask at week's end whether you trusted the one-liners and whether anything hidden bit you.

#6

The Cockpit — HTML triage surface

Defer until status unification

One self-contained HTML board rendered by tested deterministic TypeScript from structured records — never LLM prose-to-HTML, so the view cannot hallucinate. Your HTML-artifact instinct, extended from "nicer rendering" to a consumption architecture. Blocked on the queue/status unification you already scoped: the structured layer is triple-bookkept and 88% status-less today.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

Fail-visible sensors: the CLI derives facts itself (queue JSON, git diff vs Affected Files, captured exit codes); absence of telemetry renders amber, never green. Specs earn dark-green rows only after a one-time human sign-off at decomposition. Risk-ordered layout per the position-effect data; a Reaper column for undead folders; a ~500-word recurrent briefing covers what nominal work changed architecturally.

Residual risks

Garbage-in on day one without the unification migration. It solves monitoring/triage, not the 132k words of pre-implementation reading. Real maintenance surface for a solo dev: v1 must be state board + amber-on-missing + Reaper only, and survive three sprints without a caught lie before growing.

One-week experiment

Skip the CLI: have the agent generate the cockpit as a one-off HTML artifact from the current queue JSON + git log at the next session-end, with fake-nothing rules. If you open it more than twice unprompted that week, the surface has earned the deterministic-renderer investment.

#7

Strata — records are source, documents are views

Experimental · may never need to ship

The architectural endgame: typed, sharded record files with mandatory layered disclosure (headline ≤15 words / summary ≤80 / detail unbounded), markdown views generated and checksum-verified. The stance survived every attack — but if #1–#3 relieve the fatigue, this never needs to exist.

Mechanism, risks, and the one-week experiment
Mechanism

Sharded records (records/spec-NN.json, decision-NN.json — never one feature.yaml, preserving parallel-wave isolation). Assembly-at-execution feeds agents fully inlined context persisted as auditable build artifacts; a semantic tier of joycraft check spot-checks headline fidelity; a mandatory ~200-word human narrative field carries the causal layer labeled fields can't.

Residual risks

The big unknown no fix removes: whether LLMs maintain structured records faithfully under pressure, and whether an LLM fidelity-checker earns the not-reading license it grants. Highest migration cost and version-skew surface of anything in the portfolio.

One-week experiment

Run one feature with sharded JSON records written alongside (not instead of) the normal docs, generate the 350-word view, review only the view. If it was sufficient and the records didn't degrade by spec 5, the bet is live; if the agent fought the format, that's the answer too.

05

The graveyard — what not to build

Compression as the strategy (caveman as endgame)

No field that solved automated-output overload used compression — all used deletion, tiering, or just-in-time retrieval. Shorter prose still leaves you sole verifier of 100% of it.

Hard word-cap gates with "cut, do not ask"

Makes errors of omission invisible by construction — a cut safety constraint looks identical to one that never existed.

Self-reported "nominal" one-liners

The implementing agent certifying "nothing surprising" about its own work certifies self-consistency, not correctness. Silence must come from an independent verifier.

The adaptive audit lottery (quiz-graded calibration)

Quiz accuracy measures your knowledge, not the agent's defect density — it demotes to DARK exactly the areas you know well.

The attention ledger / word-metering

Unimplementable — skills are prompts with no interception point on emitted text, so "logging every human-facing word" is the model self-reporting.

JOYDEC round-trip / paste-back protocols

The highest-value review outcome — "reject both options, do X instead" — can't be encoded in a pick string; the protocol becomes decoration by week two.

Recall via git-history-only deletion

Silently returns nothing under squash merge, and agents can't grep what isn't in the tree. Archive-move achieves the same collapse safely.

The audience:agent never-read contract

Converts docs from unread-but-reviewable into explicitly-unreviewed-by-contract, deleting your only fallback when stakes spike.

Single feature.yaml store

Breaks the just-shipped parallel-safe-wave mechanism whose safety argument is disjoint files per subagent; agents reflow YAML wholesale, destroying diff locality.

AC-to-test mapping on the existing corpus

Verified impossible — real Test Plans are free-prose table cells, not named runnable tests. Prospective-only via template convention.

Session-end interactive HTML instruments

Incoherent in exactly the headless/isolated mode Joycraft is building toward — nobody is at a browser, so a markdown path ships anyway and skill logic doubles.

Audio status, 3D code cities, standalone viz products

Listeners retain complex material worse than readers; code cities won lab studies but never survived field adoption; CodeSee died commercially.

The document diet — five principles the findings force

Two-tier contract, labeled but never sealed. Human-facing docs are capped, structured, front-loaded, risk-ordered; agent-facing docs are uncapped but single-copy. Detail is human-optional, never human-excluded.

A doc enters the human channel only if it requires a human response — and "response" means a decision, not an acknowledgment, because acknowledgment-only summaries are where rubber-stamping lives. Everything else is dashboard, archive, or retrieval.

Human judgment moves to the bookends: one decision-forcing checkpoint at design, one delta-confirmation at the PR. The middle is machine-verified, exception-reported, and sampled.

Silence must be earned, never self-reported. Any "nothing to read" signal comes from an independent verifier oracled on the human-approved brief and the project boundaries, plus a standing random sample. The high-stakes carve-out (the firmware instinct) stays a written policy, not a vibe.

Archive, don't delete; delta, don't chronicle. Exhaust moves out of sight but stays greppable; session-end emits what the system became, at model-delta-proportional length. Reading load becomes a tracked harness property — the one dimension no tool in the ecosystem measures.

The honest caveat: none of this makes not-reading free. The panel's most consistent counter-finding is that comprehension debt is real — these mechanisms manage it rather than abolish it. The Theory Ledger and the recurrent briefing are the maintenance payments, and they are not optional line items.

The most surprising finding

Every mature field that solved human overload from automated systems — aviation's dark cockpit, medicine's alarm rationalization, nuclear control rooms, SRE, the President's Daily Brief — solved it structurally. Compression is the one approach conspicuously absent from every field that actually solved this problem.

Your repo confirms the inversion at miniature scale: the most re-read document lives in the smallest directory (1.3% of the corpus), while the largest directory (65%) is write-once exhaust. And the strongest precedent for Joycraft's exact architecture — HumanLayer's read-plans-not-code workflow — was publicly recanted by its own author, with the fix being a small early human checkpoint, not better documents.
